The engines

ARES is built from these programs, whose authors made them free for everyone.

ProgramWhat it does in ARES
GNU binutilsthe assembler and the linker
Unicornthe processor and the memory, from QEMU's emulator
Capstonethe disassembler of the Basic column
pyelftoolsreads the assembled program and its line numbers
Pythonthe language of ARES
Tcl/Tkthe windows

The Dynamic Instruction Counter and the textbook

The Dynamic Instruction Counter and the way it sorts instructions come from DynARMic (GLSVLSI 2026). The textbook behind the ARM X-Ray:

Harris and Harris, Digital Design and Computer Architecture: ARM Edition.

Links

  • Inspired by MARS and its tools.
  • Compatible with the ARMv7 syntax of CPUlator.
  • Built on GNU binutils, Unicorn and Capstone.
  • The Dynamic Instruction Counter is based on DynARMic: DynARMic: A Dynamic ARM Instruction Counting Tool, GLSVLSI 2026.
  • The ARM X-Ray follows the single-cycle ARM processor of Harris and Harris, Digital Design and Computer Architecture: ARM Edition.
